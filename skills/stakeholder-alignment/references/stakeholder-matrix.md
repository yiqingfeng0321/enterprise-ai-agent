# Stakeholder and decision matrix

| Decision area | Accountable internal role | Customer role to verify | Evidence required | Approval trigger |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Deployment recovery | Solutions Engineering | Technical owner | Blocker, milestone, rollback | Production, access, or data change |
| Adoption experiment | Adoption Consultant or CSM | Workflow owner and champion | Cohort, baseline, success metric | External scheduling or commitment |
| Value validation | Value lead or CSM | Business owner | Source, baseline, formula | Customer-facing value claim |
| Executive escalation | CS leadership | Executive sponsor | Risk, dependency, decision requested | Executive outreach |
| Renewal or pricing | Account Executive | Economic buyer or procurement | Value evidence and approved terms | Pricing, discount, or contract term |

## Meeting sequence

Use only the meetings needed to make a decision:

1. Working session to verify facts and owners.
2. Decision review to approve the recovery or measurement plan.
3. Executive or procurement review only when risk, timing, or commercial scope requires it.

Every meeting must have a decision, pre-read evidence, accountable owner, customer counterpart if known, due date, and completion signal.

## Escalation rules

- Do not infer decision authority from champion status.
- Escalate missing ownership before adding more activities.
- Match escalation to verified risk and renewal timing.
- Stop before external outreach or commitment until approval is explicit.
- Record unknown roles as gaps; never invent names.

## Example

Weak: “Ask the champion to approve a 20% discount.”

Strong: “Verify the economic buyer and procurement owner. The Account Executive prepares approved terms only after value evidence and leadership approval; the champion may coordinate context but is not assumed to hold commercial authority.”
